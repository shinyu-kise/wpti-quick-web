# Web 1.9.1 — clear flow and leisure activity wording

2026-10-30. Approved release candidate. Web 1.9.1-dialogue-rc.1 / Dialogue 1.5-prototype / Quick instrument v1.0.

The author found the numbered home sequence and “finish here” messages unclear, requested recognition of physically active leisure beyond exercise, and asked to remove the home audience sentence. The author then requested an interactive preview before publishing the finished revision.

- The home sequence uses three short unnumbered labels: questions, results, activity ideas. The redundant audience sentence is removed.
- Results are headed “3つの項目の結果”. Premature completion messages are removed, including for incomplete responses. The plan return button states its actual destination: “結果に戻る”.
- The leisure label is “余暇・運動での身体活動”, with an always-visible explanation of free-time activity and the existing at-least-light-breathlessness condition. Help includes brisk walking and dancing as well as exercise and sport. Travel activity remains in the second question; inactive leisure is not added to the first question.
- Question display labels, result labels, export labels, reflection prompts and metadata are consistent. Canonical source questions, option IDs and labels, recall period, scoring and domain comparison logic are unchanged.
- Following preview feedback, the leisure label explicitly reads “余暇・運動での身体活動”. Examples include after-work and day-off leisure activity, commuting and shopping, and desk work and video viewing. Childcare is included alongside work, housework and caregiving in optional context. These additions broaden relevance for working-age adults without adding occupational activity to the leisure score or changing example durations.

- The result opens a one-field optional note directly. No plan-choice selection is required before writing. Timing/life details are optional; activity examples also appear directly on the result. Explicit writing never invents a decision to change habits; previous choices, notes, details, and pending review are retained. Copy and print include written notes even without a plan choice, omitting empty note fields. This is Dialogue 1.5, separate from unchanged Quick scoring.

- First-use flow: the result shows concrete examples immediately after its domain feedback, before the three always-visible score cards. All tied candidates get equal treatment. Incomplete responses show all three general examples without prioritization; all-max responses first offer continuing current habits. Other examples are expandable. Reading an example requires no note or extra navigation. “この例をメモする” copies a draft without inventing a decision to change habits, and cannot overwrite an existing note or maintain/no-change choice.
- At common phone widths (360–999 px), the two nonresponse choices share a row to bring the next-question button into view while retaining 18 px answer labels. Narrower screens keep the choices stacked. Mobile score cards use a compact layout while retaining every domain, selected response, score/max and candidate label.

Local technical and responsive checks do not establish usability for older adults or clinical validity. The author approved this preview for publication on 2026-10-30, after final desktop/mobile and scoring checks. Deployment identity is verified separately.
